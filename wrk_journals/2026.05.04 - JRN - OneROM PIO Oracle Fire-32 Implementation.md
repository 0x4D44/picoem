## Stage 4A devil's-advocate feedback

threaded `pad_out/pad_oe` cannot remain a single local u32 pair once GPIOBASE

## Stage 4A review feedback application

Arthur asked for the Stage 4A feedback worker, explicitly leaving
`picoem-harness` to Stage 4B and forbidding commits. I used the
`code-review-apply-feedback` skill, reread `CLAUDE.md`, the Stage 4 HLD, and

Feedback evaluation:

1. Agree: threaded PIO readback published CTRL/IRQ but not `GPIOBASE`, so a
   worker-owned `PioBlock` could apply `WriteReg(0x168)` while CPU-side MMIO
   readback still returned zero. I added a published per-block `gpio_base`
   atomic to `ThreadedPio`, seeded it during handoff, updated it after generic
   PIO writes, and routed `WorkerBus` reads of `PIOx + 0x168` to it.
2. Agree: the Stage 4A threaded GPIO high/low model used separate atomics for
   low/high input and low/high PIO pad halves. That is enough for old low-bank
   paths but not for a `GPIOBASE=16` PIO-local window spanning physical
   GPIO16..47 while coordinator and PIO workers overlap. I changed the merged
   input to one packed 64-bit snapshot and changed physical PIO pad publication
   to a small seqlock over packed out/oe low/high banks.

Emotional temperature: calmer after reading the actual paths. The feedback is
well targeted; the only tension is keeping the coherent snapshot fix small
without pretending a 128-bit hardware snapshot exists in Rust atomics. The
seqlock is a little more machinery than I like, but it preserves the existing
out/oe atomicity contract and fixes the cross-bank tear directly.
