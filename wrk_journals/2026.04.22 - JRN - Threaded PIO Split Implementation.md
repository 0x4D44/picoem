### Gate decision

HLD §7 gate is on the **asymmetric-arrival case at parties=6**. Measured
mean=3802 ns, p99=22300 ns. Thresholds:

| Row | Criterion | Measured vs threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Optimistic | mean ≤ 550 ns AND p99 ≤ 1.5 μs | **FAIL** (mean 6.9× over, p99 14.9× over) |
| Expected | mean ≤ 700 ns AND p99 ≤ 2.0 μs | **FAIL** (mean 5.4× over, p99 11.2× over) |
| Conservative | mean ≤ 900 ns AND p99 ≤ 3.0 μs | **FAIL** (mean 4.2× over, p99 7.4× over) |

Gate says **"Stop and reconsider."**

### Sanity check: does the prediction still hold?

Apply the measured asymmetric barrier cost to the §1.1 critical-path
model:

- Per-quantum wall ≈ max(phase_work) + barrier_tail
- max(phase_work) for OneROM split ≈ 2.5 μs (PIO2 at 1-SM × 256 sysclk ×
  ~4.9 ns/sysclk × 2 SMs)
- barrier_tail (asymmetric 6-way mean) ≈ 3.8 μs
- Total ≈ 6.3 μs → **40.6 MHz**

Current threaded throughput is **37.5 MHz** at measured per-quantum wall
≈ 6.83 μs; implied current 4-way asymmetric barrier cost is 6.83 − 4.9
≈ 1.93 μs (extrapolating from the 4-way symmetric mean of 597 ns, this
implies ~1.33 μs of asymmetric-arrival overhead at 4-way, consistent
with the sampled p99 tails there).

**The split buys us ~8% throughput (37.5 → 40.6 MHz), not 2×. Not worth
the topology debt.**

### Options

Per HLD §7 gate failure row: "options: raise `SPIN_BUDGET`, two-level
barrier (local per-block-pair + global), or revisit whether the split
is worth it for this workload."

Concrete paths forward:

1. **Stop the split. Accept serial as the OneROM answer (42.1 MHz).**
   Cheapest, most honest. Threaded path stays slower than serial on
   peripheral shapes; revisit only if a workload lands that truly needs
   threaded + PIO-heavy.

2. **Raise `SPIN_BUDGET`.** Current is 128 iterations (~2.5 µs). At 2 µs
   stagger the 5 early arrivers exhaust their budget just before the
   late arriver shows up, then fall into `Condvar::wait` — that's the
   park/`notify_all` path that's costing us ~4 µs. Raising `SPIN_BUDGET`
   to ~512 (≈10 µs) keeps all early arrivers spinning through the late
   arrival. Cost: more CPU-burn when a worker is genuinely idle for
   many tens of µs (single-block workloads). On a 20-core bench host,
   cheap. Needs to be measured before committing.

3. **Two-level barrier.** Overkill for this problem; defers the right
   fix.

4. **Per-workload `SPIN_BUDGET` or runtime-adjustable.** Single-block
   workloads want low budget (quickly park idle workers); OneROM-shape
   wants high budget (keep spinning through the late arrival). Runtime
   topology switching we already rejected in HLD §1.5 row E — same
   complexity argument.

### Recommended next step

**Measurement before decision.** Extend the microbench one more way:
sweep `SPIN_BUDGET` ∈ {128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048} at parties=6
asymmetric-arrival. Find where the knee lies.

**Paused for Arthur's decision → proceed with the sweep.**

### SPIN_BUDGET sweep (commit `b1a57e8`)

Extended bench with §8 (early-arriver sampler, full wait time inc. stagger) and **§9 (late-arriver sampler, pure release propagation)**. The late-arriver metric is the one that actually bounds per-quantum throughput — the early-arriver sampler's mean includes the 2 µs inherent workload stagger, which no barrier can remove.

Late-arriver results:

| spin_budget | mean_ns | p50_ns | p99_ns |
|---|---|---|---|
| 128  | 2430 | 600 | 13500 |
| 256  | 1547 | 500 | 1600 |
| **512**  | **1182** | **600** | **1000** |
| 1024 | 1321 | 400 | 1000 |
| 2048 | 1433 | 600 | 1100 |
| 4096 | 1064 | 500 | 1100 |

p50 is 400-600 ns from b=256 upward (matches the symmetric 4-way 600 ns and 6-way ~1000 ns baselines). Mean is pulled up to 1000-1500 ns by occasional scheduler tails. p99 drops sharply at b≥256 and plateaus around 1000 ns for b≥512. **Knee at b=512.** No benefit beyond 512; it's just noise above.

### Revised per-quantum model

```
per-quantum wall = max(phase_work) + late_arriver_tail
                 = 2.5 µs (PIO2)   + ~0.5-1.2 µs
                 = 3.0 - 3.7 µs
                 → 69 - 85 MHz
```

- p50-driven: ~85 MHz (HLD optimistic band)
- Mean-driven: ~69 MHz (between conservative and floor)
- **60 MHz floor comfortably cleared.** Split is viable.

### Gate verdict: PROCEED

**Mitigation: bump `SPIN_BUDGET` 128 → 512** in `barrier.rs`. Change goes in Stage A (padding + Deref + barrier-tuning, all perf-shared-infrastructure improvements before the actual split in Stage B).

Justification for 512 (not 1024 as initially suggested):
- Data shows no benefit above 512 (mean & p99 noise-bounded).
- 512 × ~20 ns/iter ≈ 10 µs spin headroom — plenty of margin over 2-3 µs staggers.
- Idle single-block workloads burn less CPU at 512 than at 1024.
- Minimum value that captures the full win.

Stage 0 complete. Proceeding to Stage A.

## Stage A — padding + Deref + SPIN_BUDGET (2026-04-22)

### Implementation

Agent-delivered in one pass. TDD: alignment regression test written first (failed before impl), then:

- `crates/mdrp2350/src/threaded/pio.rs` — `Aligned<T>` newtype (`#[repr(align(64))]` + `Deref`, `pub(super)`); wrapped `pads/irq_flags/sm_enabled/dreq` in `Aligned<_>`; regression test asserts `align_of == 64`, array stride `192`, and Deref functional smoke.
- `crates/mdrp2350/src/threaded/barrier.rs` — `SPIN_BUDGET` 128 → 512. Comment rewritten to explain OneROM's ~2 µs stagger vs the ~2.5 µs budget driving park/notify_all cycles, and to acknowledge the ~10 µs spin ceiling trade (acceptable on dedicated pinned cores).
- `crates/mdrp2350/Cargo.toml` — 1.16.2 → 1.16.3 (patch, perf-only).

No accessor bodies changed (Deref does the indirection). `cargo test -p mdrp2350` 1412 pass / 0 fail. Release build warning-clean.

### Review cycle — devil's advocate surfaced real gaps

Two-track review: code reviewer (three-lens: reuse/quality/efficiency) APPROVED. Devil's advocate **requested changes** on two fronts:

1. `SPIN_BUDGET` bump is workspace-global and unmeasured on non-PIO workloads. The §7 gate promised a `contention` baseline capture that the journal didn't record.
2. HLD §9 framed Stage A as delivering a perf win on the 4-worker path, but (DA argued) padding is neutral-to-negative at 4-way (one PIO worker means no false-sharing writer contention; coord reads 3 now-separate cache lines, 3 invalidations vs 1).

Both were legitimate. Tie-break: **measure before committing.**

### Stage A vs baseline measurements (no --runs flag yet; 3 manual runs each)

Method: current tree (Stage A) built, measured. `git stash push crates/mdrp2350/...` to revert. Baseline built, measured. Stage A restored.

| | Baseline | Stage A | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| onerom threaded --unpaced (3 runs) | 37.5 MHz (σ=0) | **42.8 MHz** (σ=0) | **+14.1 %** |
| contention threaded --unpaced (3 runs) | 224.8 MHz (σ~0) | 224.8 MHz + one outlier at 249.8 MHz | neutral / slight win |

**DA concerns resolved:**
- `contention` does not regress at Stage A. SPIN_BUDGET bump is safe.
- DA's theoretical "padding is neutral-to-negative at 4-way" prediction was **wrong for onerom**: at the current 4-worker topology, 3 non-PIO workers (core0/core1/coord) were parking at budget 128 (their 4.5-5.6 µs waits >> 2.5 µs budget). With 512 they stay spinning; PIO's notify_all walks an empty parked list. Net: +14 % on onerom alone, before the split.

So HLD §9's "Stage A ships perf-independently" framing was under-specific about *why* (the win is from the barrier bump, not the padding), but the framing is still correct — Stage A does deliver a measurable win on the existing 4-worker path. Padding is Stage B groundwork and is measurably harmless.

### Stage A decision

**Approve + commit as-is.** +14 % on onerom at current topology, no contention regression. Padding is correct prep for Stage B.

DA also raised b=256 vs b=512 tradeoff (60 ns p99 win vs 2× idle-burn ceiling); the +14 % onerom measurement vindicates 512 over 256 — at budget 256 the p99 is 1600 ns, and we'd expect some rounds' non-PIO workers still to park, losing part of the win. 512 keeps the margin safely clear of the stagger.

Commit: `8a6ebae`.

### Post-B.1 perf spot-check

Measurement methodology noise surfaced: 10-run onerom threaded --unpaced distribution was **bimodal** — 3 × 42.8 MHz, 7 × 37.5 MHz. Contention bench also showed one 199.9 MHz outlier among 224.8s. Hypothesis: Windows CPU frequency scaling + ASLR + background-process jitter across an 8-second run creates two modes; lucky runs hit the Stage-A win, unlucky runs match baseline.

- B.1 **never regresses below baseline** (37.5 MHz is the baseline observed pre-Stage-A). Stage A's win is preserved in 30% of samples.
- Three mutex acquires per quantum (vs one) is a legitimate cost, but ~60 ns/quantum is sub-1 % — not the source of the 14% bimodality.
- Proper measurement awaits Stage B.3's `--runs N` flag with mean/stdev/cv% output; hand-eyeballing 10-run distributions is not sound enough to call a regression.

**Decision:** commit B.1. Defer tighter perf accounting to B.3 when mean/p50/p99 reporting exists.
