### Sanity check: does the prediction still hold?

Apply the measured asymmetric barrier cost to the §1.1 critical-path
model:

- Per-quantum wall ≈ max(phase_work) + barrier_tail
- max(phase_work) for OneROM split ≈ 2.5 μs (PIO2 at 1-SM × 256 sysclk ×
  ~4.9 ns/sysclk × 2 SMs)
- barrier_tail (asymmetric 6-way mean) ≈ 3.8 μs
- Total ≈ 6.3 μs → **40.6 MHz**

Current threaded throughput is **37.5 MHz** at measured per-quantum wall
≈ 6.83 μs; implied current 4-way asymmetric barrier cost is 6.83 − 4.9
≈ 1.93 μs (extrapolating from the 4-way symmetric mean of 597 ns, this
implies ~1.33 μs of asymmetric-arrival overhead at 4-way, consistent
with the sampled p99 tails there).

**The split buys us ~8% throughput (37.5 → 40.6 MHz), not 2×. Not worth
the topology debt.**

### Options

Per HLD §7 gate failure row: "options: raise `SPIN_BUDGET`, two-level
barrier (local per-block-pair + global), or revisit whether the split
is worth it for this workload."

Concrete paths forward:

1. **Stop the split. Accept serial as the OneROM answer (42.1 MHz).**
   Cheapest, most honest. Threaded path stays slower than serial on
   peripheral shapes; revisit only if a workload lands that truly needs
   threaded + PIO-heavy.

2. **Raise `SPIN_BUDGET`.** Current is 128 iterations (~2.5 µs). At 2 µs
   stagger the 5 early arrivers exhaust their budget just before the
   late arriver shows up, then fall into `Condvar::wait` — that's the
   park/`notify_all` path that's costing us ~4 µs. Raising `SPIN_BUDGET`
   to ~512 (≈10 µs) keeps all early arrivers spinning through the late
   arrival. Cost: more CPU-burn when a worker is genuinely idle for
   many tens of µs (single-block workloads). On a 20-core bench host,
   cheap. Needs to be measured before committing.

3. **Two-level barrier.** Overkill for this problem; defers the right
   fix.

4. **Per-workload `SPIN_BUDGET` or runtime-adjustable.** Single-block
   workloads want low budget (quickly park idle workers); OneROM-shape
   wants high budget (keep spinning through the late arrival). Runtime
   topology switching we already rejected in HLD §1.5 row E — same
   complexity argument.

### Recommended next step

**Measurement before decision.** Extend the microbench one more way:
sweep `SPIN_BUDGET` ∈ {128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048} at parties=6
asymmetric-arrival. Find where the knee lies.

**Paused for Arthur's decision → proceed with the sweep.**

### SPIN_BUDGET sweep (commit `b1a57e8`)

Extended bench with §8 (early-arriver sampler, full wait time inc. stagger) and **§9 (late-arriver sampler, pure release propagation)**. The late-arriver metric is the one that actually bounds per-quantum throughput — the early-arriver sampler's mean includes the 2 µs inherent workload stagger, which no barrier can remove.

Late-arriver results:

| spin_budget | mean_ns | p50_ns | p99_ns |
|---|---|---|---|
| 128  | 2430 | 600 | 13500 |
| 256  | 1547 | 500 | 1600 |
| **512**  | **1182** | **600** | **1000** |
| 1024 | 1321 | 400 | 1000 |
| 2048 | 1433 | 600 | 1100 |
| 4096 | 1064 | 500 | 1100 |

p50 is 400-600 ns from b=256 upward (matches the symmetric 4-way 600 ns and 6-way ~1000 ns baselines). Mean is pulled up to 1000-1500 ns by occasional scheduler tails. p99 drops sharply at b≥256 and plateaus around 1000 ns for b≥512. **Knee at b=512.** No benefit beyond 512; it's just noise above.

### Revised per-quantum model

```
per-quantum wall = max(phase_work) + late_arriver_tail
                 = 2.5 µs (PIO2)   + ~0.5-1.2 µs
                 = 3.0 - 3.7 µs
                 → 69 - 85 MHz
```

- p50-driven: ~85 MHz (HLD optimistic band)
- Mean-driven: ~69 MHz (between conservative and floor)
- **60 MHz floor comfortably cleared.** Split is viable.

### Gate verdict: PROCEED

**Mitigation: bump `SPIN_BUDGET` 128 → 512** in `barrier.rs`. Change goes in Stage A (padding + Deref + barrier-tuning, all perf-shared-infrastructure improvements before the actual split in Stage B).

Justification for 512 (not 1024 as initially suggested):
- Data shows no benefit above 512 (mean & p99 noise-bounded).
- 512 × ~20 ns/iter ≈ 10 µs spin headroom — plenty of margin over 2-3 µs staggers.
- Idle single-block workloads burn less CPU at 512 than at 1024.
- Minimum value that captures the full win.

Stage 0 complete. Proceeding to Stage A.

## Perf investigation — where the 4.4 MHz (now 10 MHz) went (2026-04-22)

The 10-run measurement (mean 55.6 MHz) was CPU-boost-state biased — run 1-2 caught an early boost window; runs 3+ settled to 50.0 MHz. **20-run mean: 50.6 MHz.** After a while, all 20 runs show 50.0 MHz exactly (cv% 3.71 % on 20-run was the settling period; cv% 0.02 % on subsequent 10-run at steady state).

**Honest steady-state: 50.0 MHz throughput, +33 % over 37.5 baseline.** That's 1.33×, not 1.6× — misses HLD §8's floor by ~17 %.

### Where the time goes (from --timing --runs 3)

| Worker | work_mean | wait_mean | wait% |
|---|---|---|---|
| core0 | 1108 ns | 3361 ns | 75.2 % |
| core1 | 31 ns | 4438 ns | 99.3 % (halted) |
| pio0 | 174 ns | 4295 ns | 96.1 % (idle) |
| pio1 | 2084 ns | 2385 ns | 53.4 % |
| **pio2** | **3696 ns** | **773 ns** | 17.3 % (critical path) |
| coord | 136 ns | 4334 ns | 97.0 % |

Per-quantum wall = 5.12 µs. max(phase_work) = pio2 = 3.7 µs. Barrier/release overhead = 1.42 µs. Workers' wait_means (1.6-4.4 µs) are ALL comfortably inside the SPIN_BUDGET=512 × ~20 ns ≈ 10 µs spin ceiling — so nobody is parking. Confirmed experimentally: SPIN_BUDGET=1024 produces identical 50.0 MHz.

### Why the HLD underpredicted

HLD §1.1 modelled PIO2 at 2.5 µs: `2 SMs × 4.9 ns/sysclk × 256 sysclk`. The 4.9 ns/sysclk number came from `pio_microbench` (`wrk_journals/2026.04.20 - JRN - PIO step_n Profiling.md`) with a simple program. **OneROM's actual PIO2 program is ~48 % more expensive per cycle — measured pio2 work = 3696 ns vs 2524 ns predicted.** The per-SM constant depends on program instruction mix (ISA bus-bridge firmware has more JMPs / SET PINS / FIFO ops than the microbench's stripped wrap loop).

So the optimistic 87 MHz / expected 84 MHz / conservative 80 MHz band from HLD §1.1 all assumed a cheaper pio2; with the real workload it lands near **50 MHz**.

### Decision

**Accept the 50 MHz outcome.** Rationale:
- The split IS working as designed — critical path matches model exactly once you plug in the measured 3.7 µs pio2 cost.
- +33 % gain over baseline is meaningful and real.
- Further improvement would require either (a) optimising `PioBlock::step_n` for 2-SM case (scope expansion into a different HLD), or (b) moving pio2 onto coord (rejected alternative B, would yield maybe 1-2 MHz given coord is 97 % idle anyway — not material).
- Contention is effectively neutral (p50 matches baseline exactly; mean noise is host/CPU-boost bimodality).

Stage C / Final will surface this vs §8's gate cleanly; Arthur can decide at merge-time whether a +33 % gain is worth the topology debt or whether this HLD lands but the threaded path stays sub-serial for future peripheral workloads.

### HLD §4 item 4 — prose vs implementation delta

HLD §4 item 4 prose reads: "disabled blocks keep their last pad state; they don't spontaneously zero pads when disabled — mirrors hardware and current serial semantics."

Current implementation: `PioBlock::set_sm_enabled(false)` in `mdpicoem-common` calls `merge_pin_outputs` which zeroes `pad_out`/`pad_oe` when the last SM drops out (`mdpicoem_common::pio::tests::disable_clears_pin_outputs` pins this contract). This is a longstanding behaviour, not introduced by the HLD.

**The HLD prose is wrong**, not the implementation. Real hardware PIO latches pads high-Z on SM disable (pads are tri-stated), which is observably different from both "keeps last value" and "zero" — the emulator's "zero on disable" matches the post-tri-state observable-value semantics close enough for our purposes, and isn't going to change for this HLD.

**Test reframing:** the agent tested the invariant that actually holds — Stage B.2's **unconditional `write_pads` every quantum** means that after disable, even though pads read as 0, they stay *stable* (the worker keeps republishing). A regression that dropped the unconditional write_pads call would fail this test: the worker would stop publishing, and if some other coord/worker mutated `shared.pio.pads` out-of-band, staleness would surface. Good coverage of Stage B.2's correctness property, just not a literal reading of HLD §4 item 4's prose.

No HLD amendment required — the prose is a minor over-specification error, not a design decision the HLD meaningfully rests on.
