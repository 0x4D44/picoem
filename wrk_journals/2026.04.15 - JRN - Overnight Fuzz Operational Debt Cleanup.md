## Context (one-paragraph recap)

The overnight campaign passed **~5.22 M differential instruction-level
tests with zero architectural divergences** across M33 (QEMU) and
RP2354 (silicon probe) oracles. M0+ coverage was effectively lost
because a transient Defender-style `.exe` lock at 23:39 killed the
oracle binary abnormally; the spawned QEMU lived on port 3334 after
the parent died, every subsequent batch connected to the zombie QEMU,
and the runner spent the next ~7 hours in a hot failure loop. The
three debt items below all point at "make the next 8-hour run survive
this kind of blip without silently losing coverage."

## Three debt items (from the campaign journal §Final results)

1. **QEMU zombies on ports 3333/3334.** Oracles must kill their
   spawned QEMU on *every* exit — normal, error, Ctrl-C, panic,
   `std::process::exit(_)` — so a brief parent-exe unavailability
   doesn't accumulate zombies. `QemuProcess::drop` already covers
   unwind-based exits; the gap is the non-unwinding paths.
2. **Windows process-kill runbook.** On Git Bash under Windows 11,
   `taskkill`, `powershell Stop-Process`, and `kill -9 <WINPID>` all
   hung or returned "No such process" under load during cleanup
   attempts at 00:05–00:15. The only reliable method was walking
   the PPID tree via `ps -W` and `kill -9 <POSIX_PID>`. Needs to
   live somewhere findable.
3. **`run-*.sh` reconciliation.** Arthur updated `run-probe.sh`
   mid-campaign with two hardenings: PID-qualified `.exe` copy (so
   concurrent `cargo build` doesn't collide with the running fuzzer
   binary) and deadline-read-once-into-variable (so a vanished
   `fuzz-runs/deadline` file can't stop the loop early). The
   `.exe`-copy piece appears to have already been propagated to
   `run-m33.sh` / `run-m0plus.sh` (linter?). The deadline-read-once
   piece has not. Reconcile all three.
