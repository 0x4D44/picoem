## Interpretation

- **basic (+31.2% Threaded).** Single-core ALU loop in striped SRAM,
  no peripherals. Threaded wins because dual-core parallelism is
  available on the 3-thread runtime (core 0 worker + idle core 1
  worker + coordinator), but the real gain here is amortised
  instruction-fetch/decode against the coordinator's 256-cycle
  quantum versus Serial's per-instruction interleave overhead on
  the dispatch loop.
- **peripheral (+348.5% Threaded).** Single-core peripheral loop
  (LDR/MOVS/ADDS/STR-to-SIO_GPIO_OUT_XOR) plus PIO0 SM0 wrap driver.
  Serial is 13.9 MHz — the per-instruction interleave walks the PIO
  tick for every CPU cycle regardless of whether the CPU touched PIO
  MMIO. Threaded runs the PIO in parallel on its own worker, so the
  CPU worker only synchronises at the quantum barrier.
- **contention (+121.9% Threaded).** Both cores running the basic ALU
  loop in bank 0 (16-byte apart) — exercises the +1-cycle
  same-bank contention accounting on core 1 in Serial. Threaded
  drops bank-contention accounting entirely (HLD §6.4: "Drop
  bank-contention accounting entirely in the threaded path"), which
  gives it a direct +1 cycle / access advantage on top of parallel
  dual-core execution. Both effects reinforce; +121.9% is roughly 2×
  (parallel cores) × ~1.1 (no-contention bump).
- **stress (+510.8% Threaded).** Peripheral loop on core 0 + basic
  ALU on core 1, PIO0 running, both cores in bank 0/1/2. Serial at
  10.2 MHz is the worst case — every instruction pays for PIO tick
  + same-bank contention on the dual-core interleave. Threaded at
  62.5 MHz matches the peripheral workload's barrier-latency floor
  (150 MHz / 2 → ~75 MHz on RP2350; scaled to RP2040's 125 MHz that
  sits in the 62–71 MHz range we see). The 5× delta isn't Threaded
  being unusually fast — it's Serial being unusually slow under the
  compound peripheral+contention+dual-core load.

## HLD §6.5 measurement decision

- [x] **Crossover observed** — all four measured workloads show

      +31.2%, `peripheral` +348.5%, `contention` +121.9%, `stress`
      +510.8%.
- [ ] Crossover NOT observed.

**Verdict:** **VALIDATES the HLD hypothesis on RP2040.** The
`Serial`-favoured quadrant that the RP2350 bench revealed
(`peripheral`, `stress`, real OneROM CPU-mode) does not reproduce
on RP2040 — every workload in the RP2040 bench set favours Threaded.

Keep the RP2040 threaded tree. Arthur's Stage 3b.4 guidance
("symmetry + some firmware will work best with each model") holds
independently, but this bench provides positive bench-observable
evidence — we're not keeping a Serial-only-winning tree out of
policy; every bench workload shows Threaded is the better choice
on RP2040.

## Comparison with RP2350 A/B table

| Workload      | RP2350 verdict          | RP2350 Δ% | RP2040 verdict          | RP2040 Δ% | Notes |
|---------------|-------------------------|-----------|-------------------------|-----------|-------|
| basic         | WINNER: Threaded        | +7.1%     | WINNER: Threaded        | +31.2%    | Same direction, larger margin on RP2040. |
| peripheral    | **WINNER: Serial**      | −58.3%    | **WINNER: Threaded**    | +348.5%   | **Direction flipped.** |
| contention    | WINNER: Threaded        | +98.0%    | WINNER: Threaded        | +121.9%   | Same direction; RP2040 drops bank-contention in Threaded, RP2350 doesn't model it — offset partly explains the gap. |
| stress        | **WINNER: Serial**      | −28.1%    | **WINNER: Threaded**    | +510.8%   | **Direction flipped.** |
| onerom-shape  | WINNER: Threaded        | +22.9%    | (n/a — RP2040 bench has no onerom-shape workload) |           | |

**Cross-chip pattern — the peripheral/stress direction flip is the
headline finding.** On RP2350, peripheral-MMIO-dominated workloads
favour Serial because RP2350's threaded runtime is 6 workers (core0,
core1, pio0, pio1, pio2, coord) and the PIO workers rendezvous with
CPU every quantum — heavy MMIO serialises through the barrier. On
RP2040, the threaded runtime is 3 workers (core0, core1, coord) with
**PIO running on the coordinator** (HLD §6.4: "PIO on coordinator
initially; split into workers only if Stage 4 benchmarks show PIO as
bottleneck"). The coordinator doesn't rendezvous with itself, so the
PIO tick cost is absorbed into the coord's pre-barrier work rather
than paying a dedicated cross-worker sync. Result: the RP2350
barrier-latency floor that makes Serial win peripheral/stress
doesn't apply — the PIO tick is free relative to the coord's
baseline cost.

**Implication for HLD §6.4 "split into workers if Stage 4 shows PIO
bottleneck":** Stage 4 does NOT identify PIO as a bottleneck on
RP2040. PIO-on-coordinator is the right topology here. Splitting PIO
onto its own worker on RP2040 would recreate the RP2350-style
rendezvous cost and likely flip the peripheral/stress verdicts to
Serial — exactly the wrong direction. The asymmetry between chips is
worth preserving.

## Surprises

- **stress at +510.8%.** The magnitude caught me off-guard. Serial is
  hitting its genuinely-worst-case cost (PIO tick per instruction +
  same-bank contention per core 1 fetch + dual-core interleave); the
  compounding pushes Serial down to 10.2 MHz. The Threaded path
  doesn't pay any of those (PIO on coord, no bank-contention
  accounting, parallel cores) so it runs at the peripheral-workload
  barrier-latency floor. The delta isn't Threaded-being-fast so much
  as Serial-being-compound-slow.
- **Every workload favours Threaded on RP2040.** The HLD §6.5
  measurement decision path anticipated a mixed picture ("delete
  threaded if no crossover"); the actual finding is "no Serial wins
  at all." That's a clean outcome, not an ambiguous one — the
  runtime selector still matters (some firmware may hit paths not
  covered by these synthetic benches), but the decision to keep the
  threaded tree is bench-evidence-backed, not just
  policy-symmetry-backed.
- **Threaded IQR is tight except peripheral/stress p75 = 71.4.** On
  peripheral and stress, the rep distribution was 3 reps at 62.5 MHz
  and 2 reps at 71.4 MHz (sorted [62.5, 62.5, 62.5, 71.4, 71.4]
  gives median 62.5 and p75 71.4 via nearest-rank). Likely a warm-up
  residual or OS-pacing fluke on 2 reps; noise floor absorbs it. For
  regression capture of the peripheral/stress floor, bump to `--reps 10+`.
