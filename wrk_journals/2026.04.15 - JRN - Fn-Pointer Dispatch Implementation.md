**Hypothesis (pending A/B):** pre-Stage-B, LTO had inlined every
top-level handler match arm directly into `decode_execute` (the
implementer's `llvm-objdump` pre-flight saw no `execute_thumb16`
symbol in the linked binary). The "match dispatch" we thought we
were saving was already free. Stage B replaced free inlined code
with an indirect call through a cache-loaded fn pointer — every
opcode now pays a real per-call cost. The 384 KB cache footprint
also doubles L1d pressure on the decode cache.

Per HLD §6: "If the measured saving is under the pessimistic
range, this is a miss and we revert or iterate — do not land a
negative-win change." Per HLD §8: "If (3) regresses or does not
improve, revert Commit B and re-plan."

**Before reverting**, A/B: build + measure paced_bench at commit
`12ce8c1` (Stage A — signature-only, perf-equivalent to pre-A) in
a fresh worktree, compare against HEAD. If Stage A measures
~30 h/e and HEAD measures ~37 h/e, the regression is owned by
Stage B and we revert. If both measure ~37 h/e, the regression is
system noise / concurrent work and we don't.

**Conclusion: Stage B (fn-pointer dispatch) is a net negative of
~27 % on the basic workload.** The hypothesis from §18:15 is
confirmed: pre-Stage-B, LTO had inlined the entire `match` tree
directly into `decode_execute`, so the supposed "call frame" we
were saving did not exist in the binary. Stage B replaced free
inlined code with a forced indirect call through a cache-loaded
fn pointer, plus doubled the decode cache footprint
(192 KB → 384 KB) which puts more pressure on L1d.

Per HLD v1.0.1 §6 ("If the measured saving is under the
pessimistic range, this is a miss and we revert or iterate — do
not land a negative-win change") and §8 ("If (3) regresses or
does not improve, revert Commit B and re-plan"), the prescribed
action is to revert the two Stage B commits (`8f4d2c1` and
`d2c76ed`) plus discard the uncommitted 0.5.3→0.5.4 test
additions, keep Stage A (`12ce8c1` — harmless signature refactor),
and write the post-mortem.
