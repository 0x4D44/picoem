## Goal

V2 first-pass at `--fuzz 100` produced 506 oracle_survived mutants out of
795 missed. The triage HLD identified three explanations for the
high survival rate:
1. ~89 confirmed Bucket 4 (79 dead-code classifier + ~10 vfp encoding
   helpers).
2. ~417 needing deep-pass: a mix of statistical first-pass misses
   (mutations on rarely-fuzzed encodings) and equivalent-mutant drift.

Deep-pass at `--fuzz 2000` runs each survivor through the same oracle
with **20× more iterations**. Mutations that survived first-pass by
chance flip to oracle_caught; mutations that are truly equivalent
under the oracle's observability contract stay surviving. The output
is a much sharper Bucket 4 vs Bucket 3 split.

## Design

### Input

506 first-pass `oracle_survived` records in `mutation/v2/results.jsonl`.
Split by oracle:

| Oracle             | Survivors | Per-mutant wall @ fuzz=2000 | Worker total |
|---                 |---:       |---:                         |---:          |
| `qemu_diff_m33`    | 245       | ~70 s                       | ~4.8 h       |
| `qemu_diff_m0plus` | 139       | ~60 s                       | ~2.3 h       |
| `softfloat_diff`   | 122       | ~5 s (no QEMU spawn)        | ~10 min      |

3-way parallel via the same `git worktree` setup as V2.0; bottleneck
is the M33 worker (~5 h). Total wall ~5 h from kickoff.

### Mechanism

Reuse `scripts/v2_mutation_run_parallel.sh`. The parallel splitter
matches survivors by file glob (same pattern as missed.txt sharding).
We need a deep-pass mode that:
1. Reads survivors from results.jsonl, not missed.txt.
2. Routes them through the parallel launcher.
3. Writes to a separate `results_deep.jsonl` (so first-pass results
   stay intact for reference).

The `--retry-survivors` mode of `v2_mutation_runner.py` already
supports re-running survivors at higher fuzz, but its write path
overwrites `results.jsonl`. For deep-pass we want a parallel
operation that preserves the first-pass evidence. Plan:
1. Generate `mutation/v2/parallel/survivors.txt` from results.jsonl.
2. Re-shard by oracle (same as V2.0).
3. Launch workers with `--missed mutation/v2/parallel/survivors.shard.<i>`,
   `--results-path mutation/v2/parallel/deep.<i>.jsonl`, and
   `--fuzz 2000 --timeout 600`.
4. Workers process each survivor. If oracle_caught at fuzz=2000, that's
   measured Bucket 3 confirmation. If oracle_survived persists, this is
   strong evidence of Bucket 4 / 2.
5. Merge — for every (name, classification) pair where the deep-pass
   classification differs from first-pass, the deep-pass wins (it's
   the more thorough measurement).

### Stage 2 — Deep-pass kickoff at fuzz=2000

`scripts/v2_mutation_run_parallel.sh deep-start` launched 3 workers
at 17:32 from the same `/tmp/v2_workspaces/wN` worktrees. Initial
fuzz target: 2000 (20× first-pass).

**Wall-clock surprise after 16 min**: W2 (softfloat, 122 survivors)
**finished completely** at 17:48 — **9 flips from oracle_survived to
oracle_caught at fuzz=2000**. W0 (M33, 245 survivors) was at 6/245
records — 156 s/mutant, projecting to ~10 h. W1 (M0+, 139 survivors)
was at 16/139 — 60 s/mutant, projecting to ~2.3 h.

W0's 10 h projection is too long for tonight. Three knobs to consider:

1. **Lower fuzz**: the 9 W2 flips happened at fuzz=2000 vs 100. fuzz=500
   (5× first-pass) should still catch most statistical misses —
   probability of escape at fuzz=500 if true catch rate is 1 % per
   fuzz iteration is `(0.99)^500 ≈ 0.7 %`. Tradeoff is acceptable.
2. **Skip the dead-code 79 mdrp2040/decode.rs cluster** in W1.
   Confirmed Bucket 4 from V2.0 §3.1 — re-testing them at fuzz=2000
   wouldn't surface anything we don't already know. Saves 79 ×
   ~60 s = ~80 min on W1.
3. **Drop W2's already-completed deep-pass results** — they're at
   fuzz=2000, the gold standard. Preserve.

Decision: stop W0/W1, restart at fuzz=500 with the 79 dead-code
cluster excluded from W1's shard. W2's fuzz=2000 results stay.

statistical misses**. That validates V2's first-pass at fuzz=100
having a ~7 % flip rate — small but non-zero. The 113 persistent
softfloat survivors are now strong Bucket 4 candidates: either
encoding helpers (vfp_sd/sn/sm/fpscr_set_nzcv, ~10) or
softfloat-equivalent IEEE-754 mutations (e.g. corner-case rounding
that softfloat_diff doesn't generate, ~100).
